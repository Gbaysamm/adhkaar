Why Adhkaar exists

We know the adhkaar.
The phone wins anyway.

Every morning the reminder arrives in a WhatsApp group. We know what the adhkaar are worth, and that they take only a few minutes. Then we pick up the phone for something else, and the time is gone.

A reminder you can swipe away doesn't change that. What helps is the adhkaar themselves, on your screen, the moment the phone is in your hand.

Before

A reminder in a group chat, and a promise to do it later.

With Adhkaar

The adhkaar open on your screen, ready to read, with the counter waiting.

Always on your phone?

The adhkaar open as soon as they're due, and in Lockdown the other apps wait until you've read them.

Hardly touch it?

Whenever you pick it up during the adhkaar time, they're the first thing you see, until they're done or the time ends.

How it helps

The adhkaar come to you.
You don't have to remember.

Set your masjid's times once. After that the adhkaar arrive when you've just prayed and your phone is in your hand, the moment they usually slip away.

  1. Once

    Set your masjid's times

    From then on you never think about when; the app follows the times you pray.

  2. 6:00 AM · 4:45 PM

    They open after salah

    Nothing to search for. Tap to count, and each dhikr moves on by itself.

  3. About 7 minutes later

    You see the habit grow

    Each session adds to your streak and your month, so a missed day is something you notice.

Questions

Asked, and answered.

Still wondering about something? Ask in the app under Settings › Help › Contact us.

Who is Adhkaar for?

People who want to keep the morning and evening adhkaar but lose them to a busy phone: the few minutes after Fajr and after Asr are when most of us are scrolling, not working. That's exactly when the adhkaar open.

If you already keep them easily, Gentle mode is a simple reminder. If the phone keeps winning, Full screen and Lockdown help more.

How is it different from apps that just send a notification?

A notification is easy to swipe away. Adhkaar can open the adhkaar on your screen by themselves, even on the lock screen (Full screen), or pause your other apps until you've read them (Lockdown). You choose how firm it is, and you can change it any time it isn't active.

What if I'm not holding my phone when the time comes?

The alert rings for a minute, and once more ten minutes later. After that it stays quiet, and in Full screen and Lockdown the adhkaar are there the next time you unlock your phone, until you finish or the adhkaar time ends (7:30 AM in the morning, Isha in the evening).

Isn't blocking my phone forcing me? Does that build a habit?

Blocking alone doesn't build a habit, and the app isn't built on it. It's built to make the adhkaar easy to start at the right moment: they open right after you pray, with the counter ready, and each dhikr moves on by itself.

It also shows how little is left, keeps your streak and your month in view, and asks gently, with a hadith, before you pause. Lockdown is there for the days the phone keeps winning, and you choose it yourself.

Can I just close it?

In Gentle, yes. In Full screen, yes, but it comes back the next time you unlock, until you finish or the time ends. In Lockdown there's no close button: you can take a break of 10, 20, 30 or 60 minutes, after a five-second hold, and the adhkaar come back when it ends.

Can people get around Lockdown?

Someone determined can always uninstall an app, and Adhkaar doesn't try to stop that. What it does is remove the easy escapes: no close button, back doesn't work, and the mode can't be changed while Lockdown is on. What's left is a pause, which still brings the adhkaar back today.

What about calls and emergencies?

They always work. Even in Lockdown, "Call or emergency" opens your phone app and it stays open for as long as you need it. Incoming calls are never blocked.

Does it follow my masjid's times?

Yes. It starts with common local times, and you set your masjid's times in Settings › Salah reminders. The morning adhkaar open about ten minutes after the congregation for Fajr, and the evening adhkaar about thirty minutes after Asr is prayed.

Where do the adhkaar and reminders come from?

The adhkaar are from Hisn al-Muslim with their sources shown. The daily reminders are verses and hadith from Tanzil, QuranEnc and HadeethEnc, shown exactly as published. Any change to religious text needs a content reviewer's approval and a cited source.

Is my data private? Does it need the internet?

There are no ads, no account and no tracking, and nothing leaves your phone. It works offline; the only thing it downloads is the Nigerian moon-sighting list for the Hijri calendar.

Is it free? Is there an iPhone version?

It's free, with no ads and nothing to buy. It's on Android now in testing, Google Play comes next, and an iPhone version is planned. Until then, anyone can try it in the browser simulator.

A reminder didn't come. What do I do?

Some phones stop apps in the background to save battery. Open Settings › Help › Test kit: it checks the permissions reminders need and lets you try every alert in ten seconds. If something's still wrong, Contact us in the same place sends your message with your phone's details.
